# SOLACE — Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0  
**Brand line:** *Power when it matters.*  
**Descriptor:** Portable Emergency Energy & Communication System  
**Visual concept:** The Beacon — Protective Light  
**Status:** Foundational identity for the SOLACE prototype

---

## 1. Brand foundation

### Name and meaning

**SOLACE** means comfort, relief, or consolation during sadness, distress, or worry. The name expresses the human purpose of the product: helping people maintain access to essential energy, light, and information during an emergency.

### Purpose

To provide a compact, accessible source of essential energy and communication when ordinary infrastructure may be unavailable.

### Brand promise

SOLACE brings together **light, power, and information** in one portable emergency device.

### Positioning

A practical, human-centred emergency-support device. It is a piece of useful engineering, not a military product, medical device, or generic power bank.

### Values

- **Reliability:** clear functions, understandable status, and careful engineering.
- **Preparedness:** designed around foreseeable emergency needs.
- **Clarity:** simple controls, labels, and visual hierarchy.
- **Human focus:** technology that supports people in stressful situations.
- **Restraint:** no unnecessary visual complexity or unsupported claims.

### Slogan

**Power when it matters.**

Use the slogan in the primary brand lockup, presentation covers, packaging, and introductory product descriptions. Do not force it into small labels, controls, or technical diagrams where it reduces legibility.

## 2. Logo concept — The Beacon

The symbol is a **protective arch surrounding a light core**.

- **Outer arch:** protection, shelter, and a dependable structure.
- **Central circle:** a source of light and a point of focus.
- **Open lower area:** accessibility and the outward delivery of help.
- **Simple silhouette:** recognisable at small sizes and suitable for printing or marking on an enclosure.

The symbol is deliberately abstract. It should suggest a beacon and protection without becoming a literal house, shield, medical cross, or emergency-services insignia.

### Master assets

- `SOLACE_Logo.png` — standalone symbol, transparent background.
- `SOLACE_Brand_Board.png` — primary lockup, slogan, and palette overview.
- `SOLACE_Logo_Master.svg` — vector master for scaling and future production.

The SVG is the preferred master for manufacturing, laser marking, and large-format use. Use PNG for raster workflows.

### Logo construction

The master symbol uses a square 500 × 500 viewBox. The arch and core are centred on the vertical axis. Preserve the relationship between the outer arch and central circle; do not independently stretch, rotate, skew, or reposition either element.

### Clear space

Use a minimum clear space equal to the diameter of the central circle on every side of the symbol or lockup. Keep text, borders, fasteners, seams, and other graphics outside this area.

### Minimum size

Recommended starting points (verify on the actual manufacturing process):

- Symbol: 12 mm wide for print or enclosure marking.
- Digital symbol: 24 px wide.
- Full lockup: 100 mm wide in print or 220 px wide digitally.

If the symbol is smaller than these sizes, test a physical or on-screen sample before release. Do not allow the central circle or arch opening to close up.

### Approved variants

1. **Primary:** Deep Resilience arch with Renewal Teal core on Soft White or Warm Stone.
2. **Reverse:** Soft White symbol on Deep Resilience background. Use a single-color version when reproduction requires it.
3. **Monochrome:** one solid colour, preferably Graphite or Deep Resilience, for engraving, embossing, stamps, and low-cost printing.
4. **Symbol-only:** use where space is constrained, such as a device corner, app icon, or status screen.

### Do not

- Add gradients, bevels, glows, shadows, or 3D effects.
- Change the arch thickness or the central circle size.
- Use outlines instead of the solid master shape.
- Place the logo over a busy photograph or low-contrast surface.
- Add extra rays, pulses, medical crosses, flames, or warning triangles to the master symbol.
- Recolour the symbol with status or safety colours to imply a device state.
- Rebuild the wordmark using an unrelated display font.

## 3. Colour system

The palette is intentionally restrained: a dark, stable primary; a calm teal accent; warm neutrals; a legible dark text colour; and a limited amber accent.

| Token | HEX | RGB | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deep Resilience | `#173B43` | `23, 59, 67` | Primary brand colour, wordmark, headers, dark surfaces |
| Renewal Teal | `#4C9B8B` | `76, 155, 139` | Secondary brand colour, logo core, selected highlights |
| Warm Stone | `#E8E6DF` | `232, 230, 223` | Warm neutral backgrounds, cards, packaging |
| Soft White | `#F8F8F5` | `248, 248, 245` | Main light background and reverse-logo field |
| Graphite | `#252D30` | `37, 45, 48` | Body text, technical labels, high-contrast details |
| Signal Amber | `#E2A64B` | `226, 166, 75` | Limited attention accent, non-critical highlights |

### Suggested proportions

For general brand compositions, use the following as a starting point, not a rigid rule:

- 55–70% Soft White or Warm Stone.
- 20–30% Deep Resilience.
- 5–12% Renewal Teal.
- 0–5% Signal Amber.
- Graphite for text and technical information as needed.

### Colour use

- Use Deep Resilience for the main wordmark, headings, and dark product surfaces.
- Use Renewal Teal as an accent, not as the default colour for small text on a light background.
- Use Graphite for body text on light backgrounds.
- Use Soft White and Warm Stone to create calm, uncluttered layouts.
- Reserve Signal Amber for a small number of highlights. Do not use it as decoration everywhere.

### Contrast and accessibility

Check text contrast for every actual foreground/background pairing. Prefer Graphite or Deep Resilience text on light backgrounds. White text on Renewal Teal and small text in Amber must be contrast-tested rather than assumed accessible. For interface text, aim for WCAG 2.2 AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Do not rely on colour alone to communicate a device state; pair colour with a label, icon, or pattern.

### Industrial and safety colour distinction

The brand palette is **not** a safety-colour specification. Do not interpret Renewal Teal as a certified safe-condition signal or Signal Amber as a substitute for a compliant warning indicator. Where formal safety signs or equipment status indicators are required, follow the applicable standards and the system's engineering requirements. ISO 3864-1 defines safety-colour and design principles; ISO 7010 specifies registered safety signs. The brand logo must never replace a required safety symbol.

## 4. Typography

### Brand typography

- **Manrope SemiBold / Bold:** preferred wordmark and major headings.
- **Inter Regular / Medium / SemiBold:** UI, body text, captions, technical labels, tables, and documentation.

Use Manrope for display moments and Inter for information that must be read quickly. If Manrope is unavailable, use Inter Display SemiBold as a practical substitute. Do not mix several unrelated font families.

### Typographic hierarchy

| Level | Typeface | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | Manrope | Bold / custom-adjusted | SOLACE logo lettering |
| H1 | Manrope | Bold | Document or screen title |
| H2 | Manrope | SemiBold | Section heading |
| Body | Inter | Regular | Paragraphs and explanations |
| UI label | Inter | Medium / SemiBold | Buttons, controls, indicators |
| Technical data | Inter | Medium | Units, specifications, tables |

Use sentence case for most headings and labels. Use uppercase sparingly for short technical identifiers. Avoid long all-caps paragraphs.

## 5. Graphic language

### Shapes

Use simple geometry derived from the logo: arches, circles, rounded rectangles, and clear alignment. Prefer consistent corner radii and a small spacing scale. Avoid ornamental patterns that compete with controls or instructions.

### Iconography

- Use simple, consistent line or solid icons from one icon family.
- Keep icons recognisable at small sizes.
- Pair unfamiliar icons with short text labels.
- Use universally understood symbols only when their meaning is clear in context.
- Do not invent safety symbols or imitate official emergency-service insignia.

### Photography and renders

Show the actual product in realistic, uncluttered environments. Use natural lighting and neutral backgrounds. Emergency context should be respectful and non-sensational. Do not depict disasters merely as dramatic decoration. Product renders must not imply features that have not been implemented or tested (for example, waterproofing, impact certification, or a runtime beyond measured results).

### Layout

- Use a clear grid and consistent alignment.
- Keep generous whitespace.
- Prefer short labels and direct instructions.
- Use colour as a hierarchy aid, not as the only source of meaning.
- Prioritise legibility under low-light and stressful-use conditions.

## 6. Product and interface applications

The project brief describes a portable emergency-support prototype with three principal functions: illumination, phone charging, and AM radio reception. It also requires system-state indication and at least one additional sensor or transducer whose reading is used by the system.

### Enclosure

- Place the symbol where it does not compete with controls, vents, connectors, or safety labels.
- Use a single-colour mark for engraving or moulded/printed enclosure details when colour reproduction is impractical.
- Keep the product name visible but secondary to essential control labels.
- Use durable, high-contrast labels for power, charging, lighting, radio, and service access.
- Do not let brand graphics obscure battery access, polarity, ratings, or warnings.

### Interface and indicators

- Present essential state information using text plus an icon or other redundant cue.
- Distinguish normal operation, charging, low energy, and fault conditions clearly.
- Use the amber accent sparingly; critical state colours must be chosen according to the actual electrical safety and usability design, not merely the brand palette.
- Ensure indicators remain legible in both bright and low-light environments.

### Packaging and documentation

Use Soft White or Warm Stone as the main field, Deep Resilience for headings, and Renewal Teal for restrained accents. Place the full lockup on covers and the symbol-only mark on small labels. Technical diagrams should prioritise functional clarity over decoration.

## 7. Project facts and claims control

The source project brief supplied for this identity is titled **“ARES – Almacenamiento y respaldo energético para sismos”** (Proyecto Final Materiales IEE, 2026-20). SOLACE is the selected brand name for the prototype; it does not change the engineering requirements in that brief.

The brief specifies, among other things:

- Maximum assembled dimensions: **150 × 150 × 80 mm**.
- Maximum assembled mass: **1 kg**.
- Main subsystems: LED illumination, regulated 5 V phone charging, and an AM radio receiver with tuning and volume control.
- Minimum autonomy scenario: LED illumination for 2 hours, phone charging for 30 minutes, and AM radio for 5 hours, with the subsystems operating simultaneously as described in the brief.
- A battery protection system and USB-based charging.
- At least one additional sensor or transducer whose reading is used by the system, plus state indication.

These are requirements from the supplied course brief, not proof that the final prototype already meets them. Do not describe the product as certified, waterproof, shockproof, medically approved, or validated to a particular runtime unless the team has evidence for that claim.

## 9. Prompt for future design models

Use the following prompt as the baseline when generating new SOLACE assets:

> Create a visual asset for SOLACE, a compact emergency energy and communication device. Follow the SOLACE brand identity exactly: minimalist geometric design; Deep Resilience `#173B43`, Renewal Teal `#4C9B8B`, Warm Stone `#E8E6DF`, Soft White `#F8F8F5`, Graphite `#252D30`, and limited Signal Amber `#E2A64B`; Manrope for display typography and Inter for interface and body text. The logo is a protective arch surrounding a small circular light core. Preserve its proportions and clear space. The slogan is “Power when it matters.” Use generous whitespace, strong contrast, simple shapes, and clear hierarchy. Do not add gradients, shadows, unnecessary decoration, fake certifications, unverified technical claims, or official safety symbols. Make the result readable, practical, and suitable for an emergency-support product.

### Asset-specific additions

- **Product render:** show only features confirmed by the engineering design; keep controls and labels realistic.
- **UI:** prioritise state clarity, large readable labels, and redundant cues beyond colour.
- **Technical report:** use restrained colour, clean diagrams, consistent units, and compact tables.
- **Packaging:** use the full logo lockup on the main face and a symbol-only mark on secondary faces.
- **Social/presentation graphic:** use the slogan only when there is sufficient space and the message remains clear.

## 10. Final brand checklist

Before publishing any asset, verify:

- [ ] The SOLACE name and slogan are spelled exactly.
- [ ] The Beacon symbol proportions have not been changed.
- [ ] Only approved brand colours are used for brand elements.
- [ ] Text contrast is sufficient for its size and use.
- [ ] Colour is not the only way to convey a status or warning.
- [ ] Safety markings remain distinct from brand decoration.
- [ ] No unverified performance, durability, or certification claims appear.
- [ ] The design remains legible at the intended physical or digital size.
- [ ] The result feels calm, clear, dependable, and human-centred.
